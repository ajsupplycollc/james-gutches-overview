### 1. SAMHSA BCOR — Building Communities of Recovery
**What you said:** Up to $300K/yr, for recovery community organizations.

**Verdict: CAUTION**

**What's accurate:**
- The program is real and $300K/year per award is the correct cap (direct + indirect costs).
- It is genuinely targeted at recovery community organizations (RCOs), not clinical treatment providers.

**What needs correction or flagging:**
- The most recent NOFO is TI-24-003, which had a deadline of April 29, 2024. It is **closed**. There is no confirmed open FY2025 or FY2026 NOFO as of May 2026.
- The FY2026 SAMHSA budget is under significant political pressure. The House budget eliminates BCOR entirely; the Senate restores it. The program is listed as funded at roughly half last year's level as a midpoint estimate. The outcome is unresolved.
- The NOFO on samhsa.gov is flagged as posted pre-January 20, 2025 and "remains for historical purposes" — that is a red flag that the current administration may not reissue it.
- Jimmy Safe Place pursuing FARR accreditation is aligned with what this grant targets, but they would need to apply as a recovery community organization, not just a housing provider.

**Safe to present?** Present it as a **program to monitor** with a clear note that no active NOFO exists and funding is subject to federal budget negotiations. Do NOT present it as an open opportunity.

### 2. Florida Opioid Settlement Abatement Funds
**What you said:** $17.8M statewide pool for recovery housing.

**Verdict: CAUTION**

**What's accurate:**
- Florida's Statewide Council on Opioid Abatement (SCOA) does allocate opioid settlement funds, and recovery housing is a documented use category.
- The $17.8M figure appears in the FY2024-25 SCOA annual report as a total allocation for recovery housing across all sources.

**What needs correction:**
- The actual recurring DCF budget for recovery housing under this program is approximately **$8.72M**, not $17.8M. The higher figure includes one-time appropriations and non-recurring dollars. Presenting $17.8M as "the pool" overstates available recurring funding.
- **Critical structural point:** This is NOT a direct grant application process for nonprofits. Funds flow from state to DCF → to Managing Entities (like BBHC in Broward) → to contracted providers. A new organization cannot simply apply for opioid settlement housing money; they must become a contracted provider through the local managing entity.
- Counties and municipalities have separate direct allocations (local settlement shares), which can be accessed at the county level — but that process varies by county and is not a standardized open RFP.

**Safe to present?** Yes, but reframe it: "Florida allocates significant opioid settlement dollars to recovery housing; the pathway for Jimmy Safe Place is through BBHC (managing entity) provider contracting, not a standalone grant application." Do not quote the $17.8M as an accessible grant pool.

### 3. HUD Continuum of Care (CoC)
**What you said:** $100K–$400K for transitional housing serving homeless.

**Verdict: CAUTION**

**What's accurate:**
- The CoC program is real, well-funded, and HUD is currently rebalancing it **toward** transitional housing (a recent policy shift, confirmed May 2026).
- Sober living homes CAN qualify if they serve homeless or at-risk homeless individuals, operate on a time-limited (12–24 month) transitional model, and deliver wraparound supportive services.
- The FY2025 NOFO deadline was February 9, 2026 (now closed). The FY2026 NOFO is expected by June 1, 2026, with awards by December 1, 2026.

**What needs correction:**
- The $100K–$400K dollar range was **not verifiable from official HUD sources**. CoC grants vary enormously by project type, local CoC, and whether it's a new or renewal project. Some awards are well above $400K; some are below $100K. The range may have come from a third-party blog. Do not present this as an official figure.
- **Key structural issue:** Jimmy Safe Place cannot apply directly to HUD. Applications go through the **local Continuum of Care** — for Broward County, that is the Broward County Continuum of Care (administered through Broward County's Human Services Department). The organization must be accepted into the CoC, participate in the annual competition process, and submit through that body.
- They also must be serving people who are homeless or at risk of homelessness — if residents are already housed and stable when they arrive at Jimmy Safe Place, CoC eligibility gets complicated.

**Safe to present?** Yes, but remove the dollar range and clarify the application pathway: "CoC funding is accessed through Broward County CoC — Jimmy Safe Place should engage the CoC now, ahead of the FY2026 competition."

### 4. Petco Love
**What you said:** $5K–$750K, next cycle September 2026.

**Verdict: FAIL**

**What's wrong:**
- The **maximum confirmed grant amount is $100,000**, not $750,000. This was confirmed directly from the Petco Love grants page and multiple third-party sources. No documentation of a $750K award category was found anywhere. The $5K floor is accurate for some programs.

- PAWsitive Recovery Inc would need to apply as an animal welfare organization, not a spay/neuter clinic, which means the next realistic application window is **February–March 2027**.

**Safe to present?** Fix the amount to $5K–$100K and correct the cycle date to February–March 2027 for PAWsitive Recovery's applicable program. If the $750K figure stays in the deck, it will be immediately fact-checked and destroy credibility.

### 6. HABRI — Human Animal Bond Research Institute
**What you said:** ~$50K, requires research component + university partner.

**Verdict: CAUTION**

**What's accurate:**
- The program is real and active. ~$50K average award is confirmed by HABRI's own documentation (5–6 projects per year, averaging ~$50K each, ~20-month duration).
- The requirement for IRB/IACUC review is accurate — this is a research grant, not a program grant.

**What needs correction:**
- A **university affiliation is not explicitly required** by HABRI. Academic institutions are listed as eligible alongside nonprofits, and international organizations can apply. However, IRB review typically requires institutional affiliation (university or hospital), so in practice, most recipients are academic-adjacent. A small nonprofit without a research infrastructure partner would face significant barriers — the "university partner" framing isn't officially wrong, just not stated as a hard requirement.
- **The 2026 cycle is closed.** Deadline was February 26, 2026; decisions expected August 2026. The **next opportunity is likely the 2027 cycle**, which would open late 2026.
- This grant is best suited to PAWsitive Recovery if they partner with a university (FIU, UM, FAU, Nova Southeastern) to co-author a proposal on the therapeutic outcomes of animal-assisted recovery — that is a legitimate and compelling research angle, but it requires 6–12 months of partnership development first.

**Safe to present?** Yes, with the caveat that the current cycle is closed and a university research partnership needs to be built before the 2027 cycle. Present it as a medium-term opportunity.

**What you said:** Inquiry-based, multi-year grants for Broward/Miami-Dade/Monroe nonprofits.

- 501(c)(3) status required; projects must exclusively serve residents of the three-county area.

**What to note:**
- Dollar amounts are not published — HFSF says "award amounts vary depending on the funding program." Do not assign a number to this in the presentation.
- Whether substance use recovery and sober living specifically align with their current funding priorities is not documented publicly. Their FAQ does not address it. Recommend a pre-submission call with program staff before including it in a formal proposal.
- The "Healthy & Thriving Communities" framework they support could encompass recovery housing, but it's not confirmed. Flag as "strong fit potential — requires pre-submission inquiry."

**Safe to present?** Yes, as written. Just don't attach a dollar figure.

### 9. Broward Behavioral Health Coalition (BBHC)
**What you said:** Distributes DCF/SOR funds in Broward via rolling RFPs.

**Verdict: CAUTION**

**What's accurate:**
- BBHC is real and is officially designated by DCF as Broward's Managing Entity for behavioral health services — confirmed.
- They do manage DCF-funded substance use and mental health contracts for Broward County providers.
- They have a Housing Initiative and do manage contracts that include recovery housing components.

**What needs adjustment:**
- "Rolling RFPs" overstates how open and accessible this is. BBHC issues procurement solicitations (RLIs and RFAs) for specific service gaps when capacity is needed. As of the date of this review, **no open RFPs for recovery housing services were found on BBHC's procurement page**. The most recent confirmed solicitation (RLI 24-001) was for involuntary outpatient commitment — a different service type.
- This is a **provider contracting relationship**, not a grant. BBHC contracts with qualified network providers to deliver funded services on a per-unit or cost-reimbursement basis. Jimmy Safe Place would need to go through a provider qualification process (RFA 22-001 pre-qualification appears to be the entry point based on their site) before being eligible for any contract awards.
- New providers typically need to be licensed and/or certified (DCF licensure, FARR accreditation) before BBHC will contract with them — this is actually an argument for Jimmy Safe Place to accelerate their FARR accreditation, as it directly unlocks this pathway.

**Safe to present?** Yes, but reframe as "BBHC is the pathway to sustained DCF-funded contracts in Broward — Jimmy Safe Place's FARR accreditation is the prerequisite that unlocks this relationship." Remove the "rolling RFPs" language; replace with "provider contracting through DCF's managing entity."

### 10. Maddie's Fund — Community Conversations Grants
**What you said:** Up to $10K monthly for animal welfare orgs.

**Verdict: PASS**

**What's accurate:**
- The program is real, confirmed active, and currently ongoing.
- Up to $10,000 per month is given away during Monday Community Conversations Zoom calls.
- An additional $3,000/month is available for organizations contributing to the Animal Welfare Professionals online community (Maddie's Pet Forum).
- 501(c)(3) status required; PAWsitive Recovery Inc would qualify as an animal welfare organization.

**What to note:**
- These are participation-based grants, not competitive proposals. Organizations must attend the calls and be present to win — it is closer to a lottery/raffle than a traditional grant competition. Frame this accurately in the presentation.
- The "up to $10K" is a monthly cap across potentially multiple winners — individual awards may be smaller. The $10K is not guaranteed per applicant.
- This is the most accessible near-term grant opportunity for PAWsitive Recovery. Low barrier, no lengthy proposal, just consistent participation. This is a quick win to recommend.

**Safe to present?** Yes. Strongest near-term opportunity for PAWsitive Recovery.
